## The model

Place a spin $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ on each site of an $L \times L$ square lattice with periodic boundaries.
The energy of a configuration $\mathbf{s}$ is

$$
H(\mathbf{s}) = -J \sum_{\langle i j \rangle} s_i s_j - h \sum_i s_i,
$$

where $\langle ij \rangle$ runs over nearest-neighbor pairs. In equilibrium at temperature $T$, configurations
appear with Boltzmann probability $\pi(\mathbf{s}) \propto e^{-\beta H(\mathbf{s})}$, $\beta = 1/k_B T$.
Below we set $J = k_B = 1$ and $h = 0$.

## Sampling with Metropolis

There are $2^{L^2}$ configurations, so the partition function cannot be summed directly. Instead we build a
Markov chain whose stationary distribution is $\pi$: propose flipping one spin, and accept with probability

$$
A(\mathbf{s} \to \mathbf{s}') = \min\!\left(1,\; e^{-\beta \Delta E}\right).
$$

<Derivation title="Why this works: detailed balance">

A sufficient condition for $\pi$ to be stationary is **detailed balance**,
$\pi(\mathbf{s})\,P(\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}') = \pi(\mathbf{s}')\,P(\mathbf{s}'\to\mathbf{s})$.
With a symmetric proposal, $P = g\,A$ and $g$ cancels. Suppose $\Delta E = H(\mathbf{s}') - H(\mathbf{s}) > 0$. Then

$$
\frac{A(\mathbf{s}\to\mathbf{s}')}{A(\mathbf{s}'\to\mathbf{s})} = \frac{e^{-\beta\Delta E}}{1}
= \frac{\pi(\mathbf{s}')}{\pi(\mathbf{s})}.
$$

The case $\Delta E < 0$ is symmetric. Summing detailed balance over $\mathbf{s}$ shows that $\pi$ is
invariant under the chain.

</Derivation>

Flipping spin $k$ changes the energy by only a local amount,

$$
\Delta E_k = 2 s_k \sum_{j \in \partial k} s_j \;\in\; \{-8, -4, 0, 4, 8\},
$$

so the two non-trivial acceptance probabilities $e^{-4\beta}$ and $e^{-8\beta}$ can be precomputed.

## Exact result

Onsager (1944) solved the model exactly. The critical temperature is $T_c = 2/\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \approx 2.269$,
and Yang (1952) derived the spontaneous magnetization:

<Callout kind="result" title="Result">

$$
M(T) = \begin{cases}
\left[1 - \sinh^{-4}(2/T)\right]^{1/8}, & T < T_c, \\
0, & T \ge T_c.
\end{cases}
$$

</Callout>

## Try it

Start below $T_c$ from a random state and watch domains coarsen. Slowly raise $T$ through $T_c$: fluctuations
appear at every length scale, and the measured $\langle|m|\rangle$ (orange) follows Onsager's curve.
On a finite lattice $|m|$ stays slightly above zero for $T > T_c$, which is a finite-size effect.
