## Setup

A mass $m$ on a spring of stiffness $k$, with viscous damping $c$ and an external force $F(t)$, obeys

$$
m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = F(t).
\tag{1}
$$

Dividing through by $m$ and introducing the **natural frequency** $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ and
the **damping ratio** $\zeta = c / (2\sqrt{km})$ gives the standard form

$$
\ddot{x} + 2\zeta\omega_0\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F(t)}{m}.
\tag{2}
$$

Everything interesting about the system is controlled by the single dimensionless number $\zeta$.

## Free response

With $F = 0$, try $x = e^{\lambda t}$. Substituting into (2) gives the characteristic equation

$$
\lambda^2 + 2\zeta\omega_0\lambda + \omega_0^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_\pm = -\zeta\omega_0 \pm \omega_0\sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1}.
$$

The sign of $\zeta^2 - 1$ splits the behavior into three regimes.

<Derivation title="Derivation: underdamped solution (ζ < 1)">

For $\zeta < 1$ the roots are complex, $\lambda_\pm = -\zeta\omega_0 \pm i\omega_d$, with the
**damped frequency** $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. The general real solution is

$$
x(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t}\left(A\cos\omega_d t + B\sin\omega_d t\right).
$$

Imposing $x(0) = x_0$ gives $A = x_0$. Differentiating,

$$
\dot{x}(0) = -\zeta\omega_0 A + \omega_d B = v_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{v_0 + \zeta\omega_0 x_0}{\omega_d}.
$$

</Derivation>

<Derivation title="Derivation: critically damped and overdamped (ζ ≥ 1)">

At $\zeta = 1$ the root is repeated, $\lambda = -\omega_0$, so the second solution picks up a factor of $t$:

$$
x(t) = \big(x_0 + (v_0 + \omega_0 x_0)\,t\big)e^{-\omega_0 t}.
$$

For $\zeta > 1$ both roots are real and negative, and

$$
x(t) = A e^{\lambda_+ t} + B e^{\lambda_- t},\qquad
A = \frac{v_0 - \lambda_- x_0}{\lambda_+ - \lambda_-},\quad B = x_0 - A.
$$

</Derivation>

Drag the sliders to move between regimes. The dashed curves are the envelope $\pm e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t}$.
Notice that critical damping ($\zeta = 1$) returns to zero fastest without overshooting.

## Forced response and resonance

Now drive the system harmonically, $F(t) = F_0\cos\Omega t$. After transients decay, the response
is a sinusoid at the drive frequency, lagging by a phase $\varphi$: $x_{ss}(t) = X\cos(\Omega t - \varphi)$.

<Derivation title="Derivation: steady-state amplitude and phase">

Write the drive as $\operatorname{Re}\{F_0 e^{i\Omega t}\}$ and seek $x = \operatorname{Re}\{\hat{X} e^{i\Omega t}\}$.
Substituting into (2):

$$
\left(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2 + 2i\zeta\omega_0\Omega\right)\hat{X} = \frac{F_0}{m}.
$$

With $r = \Omega/\omega_0$ and $F_0/(m\omega_0^2) = F_0/k$,

$$
\hat{X} = \frac{F_0/k}{1 - r^2 + 2i\zeta r}.
$$

Taking the modulus and argument gives the results below.

</Derivation>

<Callout kind="result" title="Result">

$$
\frac{X}{F_0/k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(1 - r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}},
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{2\zeta r}{1 - r^2}.
$$

The gain peaks at $r_\text{peak} = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ with height $1 / \big(2\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\big)$, but only when $\zeta < 1/\sqrt{2}$.

</Callout>

## Seeing it happen

The frequency response describes steady state only. The simulation below integrates (2) with
RK4 from rest. Near resonance with light damping, the amplitude builds up slowly over roughly
$1/(\zeta\omega_0)$ seconds. Slightly off resonance, transients and drive **beat** against each other
before settling.

## Code

The closed-form solutions used in these figures are a few lines of TypeScript:

```ts
export const gain = (r: number, zeta: number) =>
  1 / Math.sqrt((1 - r * r) ** 2 + (2 * zeta * r) ** 2);
```
